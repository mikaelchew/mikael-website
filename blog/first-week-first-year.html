The First Week Decides the First Year: What New Partners Learn by Watching You

Mikael Chew · September 9, 2026 · 6 min read

A new partner's first week is noisy. Welcome messages, group chats, a starter pack, a training video, three people telling them three slightly different things.

Through all of that, they are quietly watching one thing: what you actually do. Whatever you say in that first week, they will copy what they see you doing.

Habits form early

If week one is all welcome posts and motivation, the lesson they absorb is that this business runs on excitement. When the excitement fades, as it always does, they have nothing to stand on.

If week one includes real work done together, such as a first conversation, a first follow-up, a first simple routine, the lesson is different. This business runs on small actions repeated, and they can already do some of them.

If commands are consistently carried out, the army will be well disciplined. — Sun Tzu

Sun Tzu observed that orders practised consistently become how an army behaves. A new partner is the same. What they practise in the first days becomes how they work.

Show first, then let them try

The most reliable way I know to coach a new partner has a simple order to it. Explain it, show it, let them try with you beside them, then let them do it alone.

  • Explain briefly. One clear reason why this action matters, in a sentence or two.
  • Show it for real. Make a real call or send a real message while they watch. Seeing it done takes away a surprising amount of fear.
  • Let them try beside you. Their first attempt should happen with you there, so a stumble is a lesson rather than a setback.
  • Step back. Once they've done it a few times, let them do it without you and review afterwards.

Three things to do in week one

  • Set expectations honestly. Tell them plainly that the early months are about learning and building habits, and that results take time.
  • Get one small win together. A first customer conversation, a first follow-up, a first person invited to an event. Small and real beats big and imagined.
  • Agree a simple daily routine. Two or three actions they can do every day, even when life is busy. The routine matters more than the size.

You are the curriculum

It's tempting to hand a new partner a pile of videos and let the system do the teaching. The videos help. They will still copy what they see their leader doing, whatever the videos say.

That's a responsibility, and it's also a relief. You don't need to be perfect. You need to be visibly doing the basics, consistently, where they can see you.

So if your newest partner copied your last seven days exactly, what habits would they have by the end of the year?
