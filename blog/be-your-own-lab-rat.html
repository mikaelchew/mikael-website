Be Your Own Lab Rat: Why I Spent 90 Days Using Everything Before Sharing Anything

Mikael Chew · July 15, 2026 · 7 min read

"Give me 90 days," I told my sponsor. "I want to become an expert on the products before I share them with anyone."

He looked at me like I had lost my mind. Ninety days without sharing? Wasn't that a waste?

I told him it was an investment. Then I did something that convinced him I really had lost it. I bought the company's entire product range, RM 2,500 worth, and started using all of it myself.

The notebook

I bought a cheap notebook and wrote in it every single day. Which product, how much, what time, how it tasted or felt, and what I noticed afterwards.

It was tedious. Some days I wanted to skip it. I kept going anyway, because I knew the value was in the boring consistency.

Around the second month I started testing combinations: which products I used together, at what time of day, for two weeks at a time. I wrote down every difference I noticed.

I also wrote down what didn't change. Some things I hoped for simply didn't happen for me. That list turned out to be as valuable as the good one.

Day 91

By the end of the 90 days I had a full logbook, notes on which combinations suited which goals, and an honest list of strengths and limits for every product. Most of all, I had my own experience.

On day 91 I invited ten friends for coffee, one at a time, and started sharing. The conversations sounded completely different from the ones I used to hear in this industry.

The usual line is: "This product is amazing, everyone who uses it loves it." What I said was closer to this:

"Let me tell you about my own 90 days with it. This is what I noticed and roughly when. This part didn't change much for me, so if that's your main reason, it may not be right for you. Here's my log. Remember, this is my body, and yours may respond differently."

Eight of those ten friends bought. That was far higher than my team usually saw, and over the following months most of them kept ordering and several introduced friends.

Why honesty sold better than enthusiasm

I was simply reporting what had happened to me. People can tell that apart from a pitch within about thirty seconds.

When someone asked how long before they would notice anything, I could tell them when I did, and when a friend of mine did, and that it varies. When they asked about side effects, I said I hadn't had any in three months, but if they had a medical condition they should ask their doctor first, because I'm not one.

I never said the product works for everyone. I said it worked for me, and that they should try it for themselves and watch carefully. That honesty built more trust than any perfect pitch could.

The man who used half his face

Years later I heard about a leader from another company, let's call him Vincent, who sold a skincare range. He decided to use it on one half of his face only, and washed the other half with plain water. He kept it up for more than a year.

By the end, people stopped him everywhere to ask why the two sides of his face looked different. He never needed an opening line again. He just said, "Let me tell you why."

I'm not suggesting you copy him. What stayed with me was the depth of his belief. He was willing to bet his own face on it for a year, which is the same thing my notebook was about.

Know the enemy and know yourself. — Sun Tzu

Sun Tzu talks about knowing the enemy and knowing yourself. In this business there's a third thing to know: your own equipment. Reading the brochure is the shallow version of that; the deep version is living with the product until you can talk about it from experience.

How to run your own 30 days

Ninety days may not suit you, and that's fine. I still use every new product myself for at least 30 days before I share it, even when my team is impatient to start.

  • Use it every day at a fixed time, so it doesn't depend on remembering.
  • Write one line a day: what, when, and what you noticed.
  • Record what didn't change as carefully as what did.
  • When you share, say "this is what happened for me", and let the other person decide.
  • If someone has a medical condition, send them to their doctor before they start.

Thirty days is a small investment. What it buys you is the right to speak from experience for years afterwards.

So be honest with yourself about this one. Is there a product you share today that you couldn't talk about from your own experience?
